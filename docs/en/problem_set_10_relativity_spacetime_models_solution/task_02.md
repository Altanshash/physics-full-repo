# Problem 2 — Length Contraction

## Given

A rocket has a proper length:

$$
L_0 = 120 \text{ m}
$$

The rocket moves with velocity:

$$
v = 0.6c
$$

The length observed by a stationary observer is:

$$
L = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}
$$

The Lorentz factor is:

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

### Find:

1. The observed length \(L\)
2. The percentage of contraction
3. Whether the observer inside the rocket sees the contraction
4. An invariant quantity
5. Interpretation of the “rocket in a tunnel” paradox

---

# Solution

## 1. Calculating the Lorentz Factor

Given:

$$
v = 0.6c
$$

Substitute into the Lorentz factor formula:

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{(0.6c)^2}{c^2}}}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-0.36}}
$$

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.64}}
$$

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{0.8}
$$

$$
\gamma = 1.25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\gamma = 1.25
$$

---

## 2. Observed Length of the Rocket

The length contraction formula is:

$$
L = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
L = \frac{120}{1.25}
$$

$$
L = 96 \text{ m}
$$

Therefore, the stationary observer measures the rocket length as:

$$
96 \text{ m}
$$

---

## 3. Percentage of Contraction

The decrease in length is:

$$
L_0 - L = 120 - 96
$$

$$
= 24 \text{ m}
$$

Percentage contraction:

$$
\frac{24}{120} \times 100\%
$$

$$
= 20\%
$$

Therefore, the rocket length contracts by:

$$
20\%
$$

---

## 4. Does the Observer in the Rocket See the Contraction?

No. The observer inside the rocket is at rest relative to the rocket.

Therefore, the observer in the rocket measures the proper length:

$$
L_0 = 120 \text{ m}
$$

So, the rocket observer does not see the rocket itself contracted.

However, the rocket observer sees external objects, such as the tunnel, contracted.

---

## 5. Invariant Quantity

An invariant quantity is a physical quantity that has the same value in all inertial reference frames.

In special relativity, the spacetime interval is invariant:

$$
s^2 = c^2t^2 - x^2
$$

This quantity remains the same for all observers.

---

## 6. Rocket in a Tunnel Paradox

In the tunnel frame, the moving rocket is length-contracted.

So the stationary observer may measure the rocket as:

$$
L = 96 \text{ m}
$$

This means the rocket can appear shorter than the tunnel.

In the rocket frame, the rocket keeps its proper length:

$$
L_0 = 120 \text{ m}
$$

but the tunnel is length-contracted.

This seems like a paradox, but it is resolved by the relativity of simultaneity.

Events that are simultaneous in the tunnel frame are not necessarily simultaneous in the rocket frame.

Therefore, there is no real contradiction.

---

# Conclusion

For a rocket with proper length:

$$
L_0 = 120 \text{ m}
$$

moving at:

$$
v = 0.6c
$$

the Lorentz factor is:

$$
\gamma = 1.25
$$

The stationary observer measures the rocket length as:

$$
L = 96 \text{ m}
$$

The percentage contraction is:

$$
20\%
$$

The observer inside the rocket does not see their own rocket contracted. Instead, they observe the outside world contracted.

The “rocket in a tunnel” paradox is explained by the relativity of simultaneity.
