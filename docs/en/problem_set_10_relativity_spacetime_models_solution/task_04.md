# Problem 4 — Twin Paradox

## Given

Twin A stays on Earth.

Twin B travels with velocity:

<div align="center">

v = 0.9c

</div>

<br>

The trip lasts according to Earth time:

<div align="center">

t = 10 years

</div>

<br>

Lorentz factor:

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)

</div>

<br>

Proper time for the traveling twin:

<div align="center">

τ = t / γ

</div>

---

# Solution

## 1. Calculate the Lorentz Factor

Given:

<div align="center">

v = 0.9c

</div>

<br>

Substitute into the formula:

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √(1 − 0.9²)

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √(1 − 0.81)

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √0.19

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

γ ≈ 2.29

</div>

---

## 2. Proper Time of Twin B

Use:

<div align="center">

τ = t / γ

</div>

<br>

Substitute:

<div align="center">

τ = 10 / 2.29

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

τ ≈ 4.36 years

</div>

<br>

So, Twin B experiences:

<div align="center">

4.36 years

</div>

---

## 3. How Many Years Younger Is Twin B?

Twin A ages:

<div align="center">

10 years

</div>

<br>

Twin B ages:

<div align="center">

4.36 years

</div>

<br>

Difference:

<div align="center">

10 − 4.36 = 5.64 years

</div>

<br>

Therefore, Twin B is younger by:

<div align="center">

5.64 years

</div>

---

## 4. Why Is the Symmetry Argument Not Complete?

At first, it may seem that each twin sees the other moving.

However, the situation is not symmetric.

Twin A remains in one inertial frame on Earth.

Twin B changes direction and returns to Earth.

This means Twin B must accelerate during the turnaround.

Because of this acceleration, Twin B is not in a single inertial frame for the whole trip.

Therefore, the symmetry is broken.

---

## 5. Moment When the Symmetry Is Broken

The symmetry is broken when Twin B turns around.

At this moment, Twin B changes reference frames.

This change of inertial frame makes Twin B’s path through spacetime different from Twin A’s path.

---

## 6. Minkowski Spacetime Interpretation

In Minkowski spacetime, each twin follows a worldline.

Twin A follows a straight worldline in spacetime.

Twin B follows a broken worldline because Twin B travels away, turns around, and returns.

The proper time depends on the length of the worldline in spacetime.

The straight inertial worldline of Twin A has the greatest proper time.

Twin B’s worldline has less proper time.

Therefore, Twin B ages less.

---

# Conclusion

For Twin B traveling at:

<div align="center">

v = 0.9c

</div>

<br>

for Earth time:

<div align="center">

t = 10 years

</div>

<br>

the Lorentz factor is:

<div align="center">

γ ≈ 2.29

</div>

<br>

The proper time experienced by Twin B is:

<div align="center">

τ ≈ 4.36 years

</div>

<br>

Twin B is younger than Twin A by:

<div align="center">

5.64 years

</div>

<br>

The paradox is resolved because Twin B changes direction and reference frames. In Minkowski spacetime, Twin B follows a shorter proper-time path than Twin A.
