# Problem 7 — Relativistic Energy and Momentum

## Given

Relativistic total energy:

<div align="center">

E = γmc²

</div>

<br>

Relativistic momentum:

<div align="center">

p = γmv

</div>

<br>

Kinetic energy:

<div align="center">

Eₖ = (γ − 1)mc²

</div>

<br>

Energy-momentum relation:

<div align="center">

E² = p²c² + m²c⁴

</div>

<br>

Electron speed:

<div align="center">

v = 0.95c

</div>

<br>

Rest energy of an electron:

<div align="center">

mc² = 0.511 MeV

</div>

---

# Solution

## 1. Calculate the Lorentz Factor

Lorentz factor formula:

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)

</div>

<br>

Since:

<div align="center">

v = 0.95c

</div>

<br>

we get:

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √(1 − 0.95²)

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √(1 − 0.9025)

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

γ = 1 / √0.0975

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

γ ≈ 3.20

</div>

---

## 2. Total Energy of the Electron

Use:

<div align="center">

E = γmc²

</div>

<br>

Substitute:

<div align="center">

E = 3.20 × 0.511 MeV

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

E ≈ 1.64 MeV

</div>

---

## 3. Relativistic Kinetic Energy

Use:

<div align="center">

Eₖ = (γ − 1)mc²

</div>

<br>

Substitute:

<div align="center">

Eₖ = (3.20 − 1)(0.511 MeV)

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

Eₖ = 2.20 × 0.511 MeV

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

Eₖ ≈ 1.12 MeV

</div>

---

## 4. Classical Kinetic Energy Comparison

Classical kinetic energy:

<div align="center">

Eₖ,classical = 1/2 mv²

</div>

<br>

Using:

<div align="center">

v = 0.95c

</div>

<br>

we can write:

<div align="center">

Eₖ,classical = 1/2 m(0.95c)²

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

Eₖ,classical = 1/2 × 0.9025 × mc²

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

Eₖ,classical = 0.45125mc²

</div>

<br>

Since:

<div align="center">

mc² = 0.511 MeV

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

Eₖ,classical = 0.45125 × 0.511 MeV

</div>

<br>

<div align="center">

Eₖ,classical ≈ 0.231 MeV

</div>

<br>

The relativistic kinetic energy is:

<div align="center">

Eₖ ≈ 1.12 MeV

</div>

<br>

Thus, the classical result is much smaller and not accurate at:

<div align="center">

v = 0.95c

</div>

---

## 5. Limit When v ≪ c

When:

<div align="center">

v ≪ c

</div>

<br>

the Lorentz factor becomes approximately:

<div align="center">

γ ≈ 1 + v²/(2c²)

</div>

<br>

Then relativistic kinetic energy becomes:

<div align="center">

Eₖ = (γ − 1)mc²

</div>

<br>

Substitute the approximation:

<div align="center">

Eₖ ≈ [v²/(2c²)]mc²

</div>

<br>

Therefore:

<div align="center">

Eₖ ≈ 1/2 mv²

</div>

<br>

So, at low speeds, relativistic kinetic energy becomes the classical kinetic energy.

---

## 6. Interpretation of the Energy-Momentum Relation

The relation:

<div align="center">

E² = p²c² + m²c⁴

</div>

<br>

connects total energy, momentum, and rest mass.

If the particle is at rest:

<div align="center">

p = 0

</div>

<br>

then:

<div align="center">

E² = m²c⁴

</div>

<br>

so:

<div align="center">

E = mc²

</div>

<br>

This is the rest energy.

For a moving particle, momentum increases the total energy:

<div align="center">

E > mc²

</div>

<br>

For a massless particle such as light:

<div align="center">

m = 0

</div>

<br>

so the relation becomes:

<div align="center">

E = pc

</div>

---

# Conclusion

For an electron moving at:

<div align="center">

v = 0.95c

</div>

<br>

the Lorentz factor is:

<div align="center">

γ ≈ 3.20

</div>

<br>

The total energy is:

<div align="center">

E ≈ 1.64 MeV

</div>

<br>

The relativistic kinetic energy is:

<div align="center">

Eₖ ≈ 1.12 MeV

</div>

<br>

The classical kinetic energy is:

<div align="center">

Eₖ,classical ≈ 0.231 MeV

</div>

<br>

The classical result is much smaller, so relativistic formulas must be used at speeds close to the speed of light.

At low speeds, where:

<div align="center">

v ≪ c

</div>

<br>

the relativistic kinetic energy reduces to the classical expression:

<div align="center">

Eₖ = 1/2 mv²

</div>

<br>

The relation:

<div align="center">

E² = p²c² + m²c⁴

</div>

<br>

shows that total energy includes both rest energy and energy due to momentum.
