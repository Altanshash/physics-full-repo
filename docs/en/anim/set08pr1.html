Problem 1 – Descriptive Statistics of a Measurement Series

Given

Measurements of a rod’s length in centimeters:

$$x = [12.10,\ 12.08,\ 12.12,\ 12.11,\ 12.09,\ 12.13,\ 12.07,\ 12.10]$$
$$n = 8$$

1. Measurement Points

2. Normal Distribution and Confidence Intervals

Calculations

$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n} = \frac{96.80}{8} = 12.10\ \text{cm}$$
$$s^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2}{n-1} = \frac{0.0028}{7} = 0.0004\ \text{cm}^2$$
$$s = \sqrt{0.0004} = 0.02\ \text{cm}$$
$$u(\bar{x}) = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{0.02}{\sqrt{8}} \approx 0.0071\ \text{cm}$$
$$x = (12.10 \pm 0.007)\ \text{cm}$$

Conclusion

The average length of the rod is 12.10 cm. The standard deviation is 0.02 cm, and the uncertainty of the mean is 0.0071 cm.

The confidence intervals are:

$$\bar{x} \pm s = [12.08;\ 12.12]\ \text{cm} \approx 68\%$$
$$\bar{x} \pm 2s = [12.06;\ 12.14]\ \text{cm} \approx 95\%$$
$$\bar{x} \pm 3s = [12.04;\ 12.16]\ \text{cm} \approx 99\%$$
