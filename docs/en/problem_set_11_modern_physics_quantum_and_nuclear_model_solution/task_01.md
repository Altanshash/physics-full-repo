# Problem 1 – Wave-Particle Duality (de Broglie)

## Given

Electron velocity:

\[
v = 2.0 \times 10^6 \ \text{m/s}
\]

Electron mass:

\[
m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \ \text{kg}
\]

Planck’s constant:

\[
h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \ \text{J·s}
\]

Typical atomic size:

\[
d \approx 10^{-10} \ \text{m}
\]

---

## Solution

### 1. Calculate the momentum of the electron

Momentum formula:

\[
p = mv
\]

Substitute the values:

\[
p = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 2.0 \times 10^6
\]

\[
p = 1.82 \times 10^{-24} \ \text{kg·m/s}
\]

---

### 2. Calculate the de Broglie wavelength

de Broglie equation:

\[
\lambda = \frac{h}{p}
\]

Substitute the values:

\[
\lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{1.82 \times 10^{-24}}
\]

\[
\lambda \approx 3.64 \times 10^{-10} \ \text{m}
\]

---

### 3. Compare it with the typical size of an atom

Typical atomic size:

\[
d \approx 10^{-10} \ \text{m}
\]

Electron de Broglie wavelength:

\[
\lambda \approx 3.64 \times 10^{-10} \ \text{m}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\lambda \approx 3.6d
\]

The electron wavelength is comparable to, and even larger than, the size of an atom.

---

### 4. How will \(\lambda\) change if the velocity is doubled?

According to the de Broglie equation:

\[
\lambda = \frac{h}{mv}
\]

The wavelength is inversely proportional to velocity:

\[
\lambda \propto \frac{1}{v}
\]

If the velocity doubles:

\[
v_2 = 2v
\]

then the wavelength becomes half as large:

\[
\lambda_2 = \frac{\lambda}{2}
\]

\[
\lambda_2 = \frac{3.64 \times 10^{-10}}{2}
\]

\[
\lambda_2 \approx 1.82 \times 10^{-10} \ \text{m}
\]

---

### 5. Will the wave effect be easier or harder to observe?

When the velocity increases, the de Broglie wavelength decreases.  
A shorter wavelength makes wave behavior more difficult to observe.

Therefore, if the velocity is doubled, the wave effect becomes **harder to observe**.

---

## Conclusion

The momentum of the electron is:

\[
p = 1.82 \times 10^{-24} \ \text{kg·m/s}
\]

The de Broglie wavelength is:

\[
\lambda \approx 3.64 \times 10^{-10} \ \text{m}
\]

This wavelength is comparable to the size of an atom, which means the electron can exhibit noticeable wave properties.  
If the velocity doubles, the wavelength decreases by a factor of two, making the wave behavior harder to detect.
