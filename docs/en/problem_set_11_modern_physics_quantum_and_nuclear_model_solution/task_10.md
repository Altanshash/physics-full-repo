# Special Relativity

## Introduction

Special Relativity is a theory developed by:

$$
\text{Albert Einstein}
$$

in 1905.

The theory describes how space and time behave when objects move at speeds close to the speed of light.

The speed of light in vacuum is:

$$
c = 3.0 \times 10^8 \ \text{m/s}
$$

Special relativity is based on two fundamental postulates.

---

## Postulates of Special Relativity

### 1. Principle of Relativity

The laws of physics are the same in all inertial reference frames.

No inertial frame is preferred.

---

### 2. Constancy of the Speed of Light

The speed of light is constant for all observers, regardless of the motion of the source or observer.

$$
c = \text{constant}
$$

---

# Time Dilation

One of the most important consequences of relativity is time dilation.

Moving clocks run slower compared to stationary clocks.

The time dilation formula is:

$$
\Delta t = \gamma \Delta t_0
$$

where:

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

and:

- \(\Delta t_0\) = proper time
- \(\Delta t\) = observed time
- \(v\) = relative velocity
- \(c\) = speed of light

---

## Example

Suppose a spaceship moves with velocity:

$$
v = 0.8c
$$

Then:

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-0.8^2}}
$$

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-0.64}}
$$

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.36}}
$$

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{0.6}
$$

$$
\gamma \approx 1.67
$$

So time passes:

$$
1.67
$$

times slower for the moving observer.

---

# Length Contraction

Objects moving at relativistic speeds become shorter in the direction of motion.

The formula is:

$$
L = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}
$$

where:

- \(L_0\) = proper length
- \(L\) = observed length

---

# Relativistic Energy

Einstein showed that mass and energy are equivalent.

The famous equation is:

$$
E = mc^2
$$

This means mass can be converted into energy.

---

# Relativistic Momentum

The relativistic momentum is:

$$
p = \gamma mv
$$

At low speeds:

$$
\gamma \approx 1
$$

and classical momentum is recovered:

$$
p = mv
$$

---

# Mass-Energy Equivalence

A small amount of mass corresponds to a huge amount of energy because:

$$
c^2
$$

is extremely large.

This principle explains:

- nuclear fission
- nuclear fusion
- energy production in stars

---

# Twin Paradox

A famous thought experiment is the Twin Paradox.

One twin travels in a high-speed spaceship while the other stays on Earth.

When the traveling twin returns:

$$
\text{the traveling twin is younger}
$$

because less time has passed for them.

---

# Experimental Confirmation

Special relativity has been confirmed experimentally many times.

Examples include:

- muon decay experiments
- particle accelerators
- GPS satellite corrections

GPS systems must include relativistic corrections to work accurately.

---

# Conclusion

Special Relativity completely changed our understanding of space and time.

Important effects include:

$$
\text{Time dilation}
$$

$$
\text{Length contraction}
$$

$$
E = mc^2
$$

The theory becomes important when velocities are close to the speed of light.

It is one of the foundations of modern physics.
