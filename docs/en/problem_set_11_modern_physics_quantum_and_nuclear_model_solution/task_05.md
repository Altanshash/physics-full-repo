# Problem 5 – Quantum Tunneling

## Given

Particle energy:

$$
E = 3 \text{ eV}
$$

Potential barrier height:

$$
U_0 = 5 \text{ eV}
$$

Barrier width:

$$
a = 0.5 \text{ nm}
$$

Comparison:

$$
E < U_0
$$

---

## Solution

### 1. What can happen?

In classical physics, if the particle energy is smaller than the barrier height:

$$
E < U_0
$$

the particle cannot pass through the barrier.

However, in quantum mechanics, the particle behaves like a wave.  
Its wave function can penetrate into the barrier and may appear on the other side.

This effect is called:

$$
\text{Quantum tunneling}
$$

So, even though:

$$
3 \text{ eV} < 5 \text{ eV}
$$

there is still a probability that the particle can pass through the barrier.

---

### 2. Barrier energy difference

The barrier is higher than the particle energy by:

$$
U_0 - E = 5 - 3
$$

$$
U_0 - E = 2 \text{ eV}
$$

This means the particle does not have enough classical energy to cross the barrier, but it can tunnel through it quantum mechanically.

---

### 3. Effect of barrier width

The barrier width is:

$$
a = 0.5 \text{ nm}
$$

For tunneling:

- thinner barrier → higher tunneling probability
- thicker barrier → lower tunneling probability

Since the barrier width is very small, tunneling is possible.

---

### 4. Physical phenomenon that uses tunneling

One important example of tunneling is:

$$
\text{Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM)}
$$

In STM, electrons tunnel between a sharp metal tip and a surface.  
This allows scientists to image atoms on the surface.

Other examples include:

$$
\text{alpha decay}
$$

and:

$$
\text{tunnel diode}
$$

---

## Conclusion

Since:

$$
E = 3 \text{ eV}
$$

and:

$$
U_0 = 5 \text{ eV}
$$

we have:

$$
E < U_0
$$

Classically, the particle cannot pass through the barrier.

But quantum mechanically, the particle has a probability to tunnel through the barrier.

This phenomenon is called:

$$
\text{quantum tunneling}
$$

A real physical application of tunneling is the:

$$
\text{Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM)}
$$
