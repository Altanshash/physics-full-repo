# Problem 2 – Bohr Model of the Hydrogen Atom

## Given

Hydrogen atom:

$$
Z = 1
$$

Electron charge:

$$
e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}
$$

Electron mass:

$$
m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}
$$

Planck’s constant:

$$
h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J·s}
$$

Reduced Planck’s constant:

$$
\hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi}
$$

Vacuum permittivity:

$$
\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m}
$$

Speed of light:

$$
c = 3.00 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}
$$

Energy of the first level:

$$
E_1 = -13.6 \text{ eV}
$$

Energy levels of hydrogen:

$$
E_n = \frac{-13.6}{n^2} \text{ eV}
$$

Transition:

$$
n = 3 \rightarrow n = 2
$$

---

## Solution

### 1. Quantization condition for angular momentum

According to Bohr’s postulate, the electron can move only in orbits where its angular momentum is quantized:

$$
m_evr = n\hbar
$$

Since:

$$
\hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi}
$$

the quantization condition becomes:

$$
m_evr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}
$$

where:

$$
n = 1, 2, 3, ...
$$

---

### 2. Formula for the orbit radius

The Coulomb force provides the centripetal force:

$$
\frac{m_ev^2}{r} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r^2}
$$

Using the angular momentum condition:

$$
m_evr = n\hbar
$$

we get the radius of the \(n\)-th orbit:

$$
r_n = \frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar^2}{m_ee^2}n^2
$$

For hydrogen:

$$
r_n = a_0 n^2
$$

where the Bohr radius is:

$$
a_0 = 5.29 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}
$$

So:

$$
r_n = 5.29 \times 10^{-11} n^2 \text{ m}
$$

---

### 3. Energy of the \(n = 1\) level

Energy formula:

$$
E_n = \frac{-13.6}{n^2} \text{ eV}
$$

For:

$$
n = 1
$$

we get:

$$
E_1 = \frac{-13.6}{1^2}
$$

$$
E_1 = -13.6 \text{ eV}
$$

---

### 4. Energy of the \(n = 3 \rightarrow n = 2\) transition

First, calculate the energy levels.

For \(n = 3\):

$$
E_3 = \frac{-13.6}{3^2}
$$

$$
E_3 = \frac{-13.6}{9}
$$

$$
E_3 = -1.51 \text{ eV}
$$

For \(n = 2\):

$$
E_2 = \frac{-13.6}{2^2}
$$

$$
E_2 = \frac{-13.6}{4}
$$

$$
E_2 = -3.40 \text{ eV}
$$

Energy of the emitted photon:

$$
\Delta E = E_3 - E_2
$$

$$
\Delta E = (-1.51) - (-3.40)
$$

$$
\Delta E = 1.89 \text{ eV}
$$

---

### 5. Wavelength of the emitted photon

Photon energy equation:

$$
E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
$$

So:

$$
\lambda = \frac{hc}{E}
$$

Using:

$$
hc = 1240 \text{ eV·nm}
$$

we get:

$$
\lambda = \frac{1240}{1.89}
$$

$$
\lambda \approx 656 \text{ nm}
$$

---

## Conclusion

The angular momentum of an electron in the Bohr model is quantized:

$$
m_evr = n\hbar
$$

The radius of the \(n\)-th orbit is:

$$
r_n = a_0 n^2
$$

The energy of the first level is:

$$
E_1 = -13.6 \text{ eV}
$$

For the transition:

$$
n = 3 \rightarrow n = 2
$$

the photon energy is:

$$
\Delta E = 1.89 \text{ eV}
$$

The wavelength of the emitted photon is:

$$
\lambda \approx 656 \text{ nm}
$$

This wavelength belongs to the visible red region of the spectrum.
