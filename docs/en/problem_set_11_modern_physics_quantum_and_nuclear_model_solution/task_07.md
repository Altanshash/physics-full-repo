# Problem 7 – Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

## Given

Energy levels:

$$
n = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
$$

Task:

$$
\text{Visualize the probability density for each energy level}
$$

---

## Solution

### 1. Energy levels

For the quantum harmonic oscillator, the energy levels are:

$$
E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega
$$

where:

$$
n = 0,1,2,3,4,5,...
$$

So the first energy levels are:

$$
E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

$$
E_1 = \frac{3}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

$$
E_2 = \frac{5}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

$$
E_3 = \frac{7}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

$$
E_4 = \frac{9}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

$$
E_5 = \frac{11}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

---

### 2. Wavefunction and probability density

The probability density is:

$$
|\psi_n(x)|^2
$$

For each energy level, the wavefunction has a different shape.

The number of nodes is equal to the quantum number:

$$
\text{number of nodes} = n
$$

Therefore:

- For \(n=0\), there are no nodes.
- For \(n=1\), there is 1 node.
- For \(n=2\), there are 2 nodes.
- For \(n=3\), there are 3 nodes.
- For \(n=4\), there are 4 nodes.
- For \(n=5\), there are 5 nodes.

---

### 3. Shape interpretation

For low energy levels, the probability density is concentrated near the center.

As \(n\) increases:

$$
E_n \text{ increases}
$$

and the probability distribution becomes wider.

Higher energy states have more oscillations and more nodes.

---

### 4. Zero-point energy

The lowest energy level is not zero:

$$
E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

This is called zero-point energy.

The particle must have zero-point energy because if:

$$
E = 0
$$

then the particle would be completely at rest at:

$$
x = 0
$$

This would mean:

$$
\Delta x = 0
$$

and:

$$
\Delta p = 0
$$

which violates the uncertainty principle:

$$
\Delta x \Delta p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}
$$

---

## Conclusion

The quantum harmonic oscillator has discrete energy levels:

$$
E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega
$$

The probability density is:

$$
|\psi_n(x)|^2
$$

Each level has:

$$
n
$$

nodes.

The ground state has nonzero energy:

$$
E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega
$$

This nonzero minimum energy is called:

$$
\text{zero-point energy}
$$
