# Problem 3 – Photoelectric Effect

## Given

Work function of the metal:

$$
W = 3.2 \text{ eV}
$$

Incident light frequency:

$$
f = 1.5 f_0
$$

Planck’s constant:

$$
h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J·s}
$$

Electron charge:

$$
e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}
$$

Conversion:

$$
1 \text{ eV} = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}
$$

---

## Solution

### 1. Threshold frequency

The threshold frequency is found from:

$$
W = hf_0
$$

So:

$$
f_0 = \frac{W}{h}
$$

Convert work function to joules:

$$
W = 3.2 \times 1.602 \times 10^{-19}
$$

$$
W = 5.13 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}
$$

Now calculate:

$$
f_0 = \frac{5.13 \times 10^{-19}}{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}
$$

$$
f_0 \approx 7.74 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}
$$

---

### 2. Maximum kinetic energy

Einstein’s photoelectric equation:

$$
K_{\max} = hf - W
$$

Since:

$$
f = 1.5f_0
$$

and:

$$
W = hf_0
$$

then:

$$
K_{\max} = h(1.5f_0) - hf_0
$$

$$
K_{\max} = 0.5hf_0
$$

$$
K_{\max} = 0.5W
$$

$$
K_{\max} = 0.5 \times 3.2
$$

$$
K_{\max} = 1.6 \text{ eV}
$$

In joules:

$$
K_{\max} = 1.6 \times 1.602 \times 10^{-19}
$$

$$
K_{\max} = 2.56 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}
$$

---

### 3. Stopping potential

Stopping potential is:

$$
eV_s = K_{\max}
$$

In electron-volts, the stopping potential is numerically equal to the kinetic energy in eV:

$$
V_s = 1.6 \text{ V}
$$

---

### 4. Does increasing intensity change the kinetic energy?

No. Increasing the intensity does **not** change the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons.

Intensity increases the number of photons, so more electrons can be emitted.  
But the kinetic energy depends only on the frequency of light.

---

### 5. What contradicted the classical wave theory?

Classical wave theory predicted that increasing light intensity should increase the kinetic energy of electrons.

However, experiments showed that:

$$
K_{\max}
$$

depends on frequency, not intensity.

Also, electrons are emitted only if:

$$
f \ge f_0
$$

This threshold frequency contradicted classical wave theory.

---

## Conclusion

The threshold frequency is:

$$
f_0 \approx 7.74 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}
$$

The maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons is:

$$
K_{\max} = 1.6 \text{ eV}
$$

or:

$$
K_{\max} = 2.56 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}
$$

The stopping potential is:

$$
V_s = 1.6 \text{ V}
$$

Increasing intensity does not increase the kinetic energy.  
It only increases the number of emitted electrons.

The photoelectric effect contradicted classical wave theory because electron energy depends on light frequency, not intensity.
