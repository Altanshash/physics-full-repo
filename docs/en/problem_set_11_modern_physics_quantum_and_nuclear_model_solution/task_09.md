# Problem 9 – Nuclear Fission and Binding Energy

## Given

Mass defect:

$$
\Delta m = 0.2 \ \text{u}
$$

Energy equivalent of 1 atomic mass unit:

$$
1 \ \text{u} = 931.5 \ \text{MeV}/c^2
$$

Einstein’s mass-energy relation:

$$
E = \Delta mc^2
$$

---

## Solution

### 1. Concept of mass defect

Mass defect is the difference between the mass of separate nucleons and the actual mass of the nucleus.

$$
\Delta m = m_{\text{nucleons}} - m_{\text{nucleus}}
$$

The missing mass is converted into binding energy.

---

### 2. Energy released

Using:

$$
E = \Delta mc^2
$$

Since:

$$
1 \ \text{u} = 931.5 \ \text{MeV}
$$

we get:

$$
E = 0.2 \times 931.5
$$

$$
E = 186.3 \ \text{MeV}
$$

So, the energy released is:

$$
E \approx 186 \ \text{MeV}
$$

---

### 3. Comparison with a chemical reaction

A typical chemical reaction releases energy of the order of:

$$
1 \text{ eV}
$$

per atom or molecule.

But nuclear fission releases about:

$$
186 \ \text{MeV}
$$

This is millions of times larger than chemical energy.

$$
1 \ \text{MeV} = 10^6 \ \text{eV}
$$

Therefore:

$$
186 \ \text{MeV} = 1.86 \times 10^8 \ \text{eV}
$$

---

### 4. Why binding energy is maximum around iron

Binding energy per nucleon is highest near iron.

For light nuclei, fusion increases binding energy.

For heavy nuclei, fission increases binding energy.

Iron is near the most stable region because its nucleons are bound very strongly.

This is why both fusion and fission tend to move nuclei toward the iron region.

---

## Conclusion

Mass defect is the missing mass that becomes nuclear binding energy:

$$
\Delta m = m_{\text{nucleons}} - m_{\text{nucleus}}
$$

For:

$$
\Delta m = 0.2 \ \text{u}
$$

the released energy is:

$$
E = 186.3 \ \text{MeV}
$$

This energy is much greater than energy from chemical reactions.

The maximum binding energy occurs near iron because nuclei around iron are the most stable.
